Architecture

Scenes, assemblies, and the engine

Integrity is a physics engine, not a graphics engine that resembles one. That single commitment decides the whole architecture — including who is allowed to know what. This page is the model, the parts, and the rules that keep them apart.

The scene sets the characters and the setting. The assemblies are the actors. The engine is the director and the stage — it knows what should happen and gets the actors to make it happen.

The three roles

Almost every architectural mistake this engine has made was a role doing another role's job: a scene computing a force, a camera exempt from collision, an appearance chosen instead of derived. The separation below is what those mistakes taught, stated so a machine can check it.

The scene may

  • place — name which assemblies are present, where, how fast
  • observe — place_camera, camera_follow
  • step — let time pass, draw what is
  • signal — say something happened at a point

And nothing else. A scene is data: yarr.html is the Earth scene plus one attribute naming a gun, a coordinate and a bearing.

The assembly knows

  • what it is made of — real catalogued materials
  • its own shape, and where its parts sit relative to itself
  • how it answers heat, contact, and the passage of time
  • what it looks like now — derived, never chosen

Nothing declares a mass. A 24-pounder's 2,375 kg and its centre of gravity follow from thirteen parts and the material catalogue.

The engine decides

  • gravity, contact, heat, chemistry — one law at every scale
  • what must be resolved into particles, and what stays cheap maths
  • what is visible, and therefore what to ask about
  • where the camera actually ends up — it is matter too

It knows about matter, heat, contact, time and light. It does not know what a cannon is.

The loop

The engine holds the clock and the state of the universe. Each step it signals the actors, they answer, and it renders the answers. Nothing in that loop needs to know what kind of thing it is talking to — which is why adding a species, a vehicle or a planet is adding an assembly.

A worked example: firing a gun

The same event, done the wrong way and the right way. The difference is not style — the wrong version puts an assembly's name inside the engine and makes the scene the thing that fires.

The wrong wayThe right way
the scene says fire_cannon(bearing, elevation) apply heat, here
who knows where the charge is the engine, by name the gun, which contains it
who decides it burns fire_cannon oxidation — heat reached something combustible
who decides the shot leaves fire_cannon the pressure, against the ball's fit
what else the call can do nothing — it is gun-shaped light a forest; the engine cannot tell the difference

In the right version nobody asks for smoke, recoil or a muzzle flash. They are consequences: over half of black powder's product mass stays condensed, which is why it smokes; the products leave at 1,950 K, which is above the temperature at which matter visibly glows, so the flash is the same incandescence that lights a meteor.

Operating principles

These were earned, mostly by getting them wrong first. When a design argument cannot be settled, one of these settles it.

Physics is the product. Real physics, not graphics that resemble it. When you are tempted to make something look right — stop. That instinct is the enemy.
One law, every scale, every scene. A raindrop on a petal, a tyre on gravel and a moon striking a planet differ in scale, material and energy — never in kind. One question must not get two answers.
Simulate what you can; compute what you can't; fake nothing. Maths sizes the interaction, the minimal necessary matter becomes real particles, the rest is real maths.
The camera changes representation, never existence. Looking away must never change what is true. The camera is itself a small shell of matter, and obeys contact like a grain.
No fudge, ever. Every number traces to physics or is an openly flagged debt that names the real computation it stands in for. If physics disagrees with the hope, record the physics.
Measure and derive; never assume. A number you did not measure or derive is a guess. A negative result, honestly measured, is a real deliverable.

What that looks like in practice

  • Colour is not chosen. Foliage albedo is 449 measured leaf spectra convolved against the CIE 1931 observer under the Sun — the same Planck curve that colours the stars. A test re-derives it every run, so the stored value can only ever be a cache of the measurement.
  • Size follows from identity. A 24-pounder's ball is 5.53 inches across because that is what 24 lb of cast iron is. Nobody typed a diameter.
  • Rules that matter are machine-checked. Prose is not a gate. The scene/engine boundary, the material catalogue, the numbering of documents and the ban on inventing a surface at a real coordinate all fail the build rather than being remembered.
  • Known gaps are written down, not hidden. A public ledger records every place the engine currently answers a question twice, or carries data nothing reads, with the evidence.

Honest state

The model above is the target, and the engine is not all the way there. Two scene structs still live inside the engine crate, so adding a scene still touches it — that is the largest open item. The boundary is enforced by a ratchet: every existing violation is written down, a new one fails the build, and an entry that no longer describes the code fails too, so the list stays true and can only shrink.

A scene that needed a capability the engine lacked used to grow a method for itself. Five such methods turned out to be five spellings of one missing verb.
