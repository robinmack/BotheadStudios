# docs/60 — Integrating Sean's fork: what we took, and every decision we made on his behalf

**Purpose.** Sean invited us to take his work rather than merge it himself, so we are resolving conflicts in
*his* code without him. This file is the audit trail of that: what came in, what conflicted, and what we
decided and why. **Every entry is a place he should be able to say "no, that's wrong" and be right.**

It exists because a merge resolution is a design decision wearing a chore's clothing. Nobody reviews the
resolution of a conflict the way they review a commit, and the resolutions are exactly where two people's
architectures get silently welded together.

## How his work is structured (and why this was easy)

He pre-split 156 commits into **nine cumulative branches**, `sean/upstream-1-ci` … `upstream-9-process`.
Cumulative, not independent: `upstream-1` is 1 commit, `upstream-9` is all nine. Taken **in order**, each step
is small; taken as one merge of his `main`, it is not:

| taken as | conflicted files |
|---|---|
| his whole `main` in one go | 12 |
| `upstream-1-ci` alone | 3 |
| `upstream-2-docs` on top of 1 | 2 |
| `upstream-3-sph-live-drop` on top of 2 | 3 |

So each step is its own PR, stacked on the previous one (`--base integrate-sean-N`), and the diff a reviewer
sees is only that step's delta.

**Authorship is preserved throughout.** Every step comes in by `git merge`, never squash or cherry-pick, so
the commits stay `Sean Reid <seanreid.mail@gmail.com>` and count on his contribution graph.

## Why the histories diverged in the first place — our fault, worth not repeating

His fork branched from our `main` *before* we merged PR #81, and we merged #81 as a **squash**. Squashing
rewrote 40 commits into one, so his branch still carries ~15 commits authored by Robin that `main` now
contains only *inside* the squash. That is what turned a fast-forward into a conflict-resolution exercise.

**Merge commits keep a fork in sync; squash merges strand it.** Now that a collaborator works from forks, the
default for upstream merges should change.

### upstream-3-sph-live-drop — "state the crossing law and retire the CPU debris leftovers"

3 conflicts. Two were the usual journals. The third is the one that needs his eyes:

**`lib.rs` — the scene-resolution const block.** His commit replaces our block
(`SPH_SHOCK_WINDOW_S`, `SCENE_DEBRIS_N`, `SCENE_CAP_N`, `SCENE_IMPACT_N`) with a single
`MOONLET_UNIS_N: usize = 1536`.

- **Decision: keep both.** `SPH_SHOCK_WINDOW_S` is load-bearing on our side — it is the quiescence gate the
  de-resolution merge depends on (docs/44 §6), and it is deliberately one constant shared with the docs/42 dt
  coarsening so "the shock is finished" has one definition. Taking his side outright would delete it.
- **Decision NOT taken, and why it matters:** `1536` is exactly `SCENE_IMPACT_N` (512 + 1024), and his own
  comment says the pool is "sized to the pool the retired CPU debris cloud used". The obvious Law V move is
  `const MOONLET_UNIS_N: usize = SCENE_IMPACT_N;` — one number, one home. **We wrote that, then reverted it.**
  Robin's correction: our side moved the disk to **GPU** SPH, so `SCENE_IMPACT_N` is a *physics particle
  count*, whereas `MOONLET_UNIS_N` is a *render pool* sized against the **CPU** path his commit retires. They
  agree only because the CPU-era pool happened to equal the GPU count we now run. Coupling them would assert
  a dependency that does not exist and would resize his draw pool whenever anyone tuned GPU resolution.
  The literal stays, with the coincidence documented at the call site.
- **Sean: if the pool genuinely should track resolution, say so and we will derive it.** We left it
  independent because we could not establish that it should.

### upstream-4-gpu-gravity — "dispatch the GPU kernels; race-free COM sweep for Metal" (PR #87)

7 files, **2 conflicts**, both `CHANGELOG.md` / `JOURNAL.md`, unioned. **No judgement calls needed — but two
Law checks were run before concluding that, and both are worth recording as things that were NOT wrong.**

1. **Is this a second answer to "what is the gravitational acceleration"?** (Law II) No. He adds a GPU
   Barnes–Hut (`GravityField`, `accelerations_tree`) and wires it into the CPU `aggregate` path, and he pins
   it the way CLAUDE.md rule 3 requires: `the_gpu_tree_matches_the_cpu_tree_within_the_theta_bound` and
   `the_gpu_tree_opened_fully_recovers_the_direct_sum`. That makes it an ACCELERATION of the CPU tree, pinned
   to its exact reference, not a rival implementation. The SPH self-gravity in `sph_step.wgsl` is a different
   container and legitimately specialized (docs/46 §1).
2. **Is `theta` duplicated between Rust and WGSL?** (Law V / `laws::SINGLE_SOURCE`) No, and the design is
   better than ours was: the shader reads `P.theta` from its params, `THETA` exists only as a Rust-side
   default, and `accelerations_tree_with` takes it explicitly. Theta has one home and flows to the GPU as a
   uniform. The worry was real — a kernel hardcoding an opening angle would silently ignore a caller's theta —
   it simply was not what he did.

Also confirmed: his rewritten `bh_gravity.wgsl` still satisfies
`pinned_constant_tests::the_shaders_gravitational_constant_matches_the_engines`, the guard added the same day
for the shader copies of `G`. `379/379`.

most valuable finding of the whole integration: **his change fixes a bug in OUR code.**

Resolved without difficulty:

**The one to confer on — `simulation.rs` hunk 3 (24 lines ours, 61 his).** Both sides rewrote the arrival path.

- **Ours** delegates the whole flight to `flight::Flight::step` — one flight law for a ground patch and a
  planet (Law II) — then excavates each `Arrival` through `matter::impact`.
- **His** keeps the loop in the scene and adds three things, all of them real:
  1. **`forecast_body_contact`** — a meteor meeting a cohesive body, forecast through
     `interaction::detect_swept`, the one collision owner. This is his feature.
  2. **A bisected impact site** — the site is where the TRAJECTORY crosses the surface, found by 16-step
     bisection, not wherever the post-step sample landed.
  3. **Momentum as well as energy** — `deposit_event(site, dir, momentum, energy_j)`.

**Finding: (2) is a bug in our code, not just a difference.** Our `FlightEnvironment::arrival` returns `to`,
the post-step position. At 17 km/s with a 1/60 s step that is ~280 m past the surface, so the impact couples
to matter hundreds of metres underground. `PlanetAir::arrival` in `lib.rs` has the identical flaw. His
bisection is the fix, and it is needed at planetary scale more than at ground scale.

**Proposed resolution (not yet applied — this is the confer point).** Neither side wins; the pieces compose:

1. **Move the bisection into `FlightEnvironment::arrival`.** The environment is what knows where its surface
   is, so it should return the CROSSING POINT rather than the sample. That fixes ground and planet at once
   from one change, and makes `arrival`'s contract honest: *where* the path met hard matter.
2. **Widen `arrival` to include cohesive bodies.** His ball IS hard matter. "Has this path met hard matter"
   should be answered by the world — terrain or ball — which puts his `forecast_body_contact` behind the
   same door instead of beside it.
3. **Give `Arrival` a momentum field.** Cheap, physical, and his `deposit_event` needs it.

That keeps our unified flight law AND all three of his improvements, and it deletes a bug we shipped. It also
**moves his code** — his bisection and body-forecast migrate into a trait contract he has not seen — which is
exactly the kind of change this report exists to surface rather than slip through. Robin's read is that Sean
would defer to us here; the reason to confer anyway is that the resolution is better if he confirms the
`deposit_event` door is the right seam, and because he may already have a view on where site-finding belongs.

**Steps 6–9 are untouched.** The camera/render architecture overlap (his `display_scale()` and camera-relative
globe against our pose API and matter rendering) is still ahead of us and is a larger question than this one.

## Steps 6–9 are PARALLEL TRACKS, not a Law II conflict (Robin, 2026-07-25)

This report previously flagged his camera and render work as a Law II problem — two answers to one question,
needing an architecture conference before integration. **That was wrong, and the correction is worth stating
carefully because it is easy to over-apply Law II and freeze work that should simply proceed.**

Robin: *"Steps 6–9 are genuinely parallel tracks; not law violation because they aren't two answers, they are
two visualization methods that should be available to scenes."*

The question Law II governs is **"where is the camera now?"**, and that must have exactly one answer at any
instant. **"How did it get there?" is not that question.** A fly camera, a scripted arc, a chase rule and a
follow are all *producers* of a pose; they are input methods, not competing accounts of where the camera is.
Offering several is no more a violation than offering both a keyboard and a mouse.

The same applies to the render frame. `vp_rel` (camera-relative) and `vp_abs` (absolute) are not two answers
about where the globe is — they are one geometry expressed in whichever frame has precision where it is
needed, which is exactly the specialization `docs/46` §1 sanctions ("the physics itself differs, so the
numerical treatment differs" — here the *arithmetic* differs). Likewise `display_scale()` vs a constant
`DISPLAY_SCALE`: a scale that varies with viewing regime is docs/13's scale-relative rendering, not a rival
definition of a metre.

**And his approach solves a problem we flagged as unbuilt.** Our own work hit the wall that one f32 depth
range cannot hold an 80 m fragment and a 6,371 km planet — dropping the near plane to the fragment made the
globe stop drawing entirely, and we bounded the ratio and named DEPTH PARTITIONING as the real fix we had not
built. His camera-relative globe, `camera_relative_eye_round_trip_is_submillimetre_at_planet_radius` and
`globe_model_translation_stays_subpixel_where_the_coarse_globe_is_drawn` are that fix. On this axis his work
supersedes ours and should win.

### Scenes SWITCH between camera systems — which is what makes it single-valued

Robin, clarifying: *"IE a switch between two camera handling systems."* That is the cleanest form of it. Only
one system drives at a time, so "where is the camera now" has one answer by construction, and a scene chooses
which mechanism it wants. Nothing needs unifying at the *input* end.

### Robin's condition, and it was NOT met — fixed here

*"As long as we can unify FOV within the engine for rendering, we should be good."* Right, and the check found
the engine failing it in the worst way. **Four projection sites, three built from a hardcoded field of view:**

| site | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| `lib.rs` `view_proj` (space band) | `0.9` literal | `SPACE_FOV_Y` |
| `lib.rs` `meters_per_pixel` (space band HUD) | `0.9` literal **again** | `SPACE_FOV_Y` |
| `ground_scene.rs` projection | `60f32.to_radians()` | named `FOV_Y` |
| `fly_camera.rs` | `DEFAULT_FOV_Y` / parameter | unchanged, already correct |

**The space-band pair is the one that matters.** It wrote `0.9` twice — once building the projection and once
in `meters_per_pixel`, the HUD's scale bar. Change the projection and the bar goes on measuring a frustum the
scene is no longer drawing: confident wrong metres, nothing failing. This is ours, not Sean's.

`ground_scene`'s literal is fixed the same way Sean's `upstream-5-ground-ball` fixes it (a named `FOV_Y` at
`FRAC_PI_3`) — converging fixes, so the merge is trivial.

**`SPACE_FOV_Y` is deliberately NOT shared with `fly_camera::DEFAULT_FOV_Y`**, which is also `0.9`. Two scenes
asking "what field of view do I render at" are two questions with the same answer today, and coupling them
would assert that one scene's framing must follow another's. Exactly the `MOONLET_UNIS_N` trap, avoided on
purpose this time.

**Guarded, not just fixed:** `laws::fov_tests::no_projection_is_built_from_a_bare_field_of_view_literal`
scans every source and requires the first argument to `perspective_rh` not to begin with a digit. A named
constant can be traced, shared and tested; a literal cannot. `380/380`.

### The one remaining constraint that keeps "both available" Law-abiding

Several producers, **one sink**. Every camera driver must end by handing the engine a single pose
(`Terra::set_camera_pose`), and nothing may reach around it to mutate camera state directly. If his `arc_*`
path writes to `fly` while `set_camera_pose` also exists, then "where is the camera" genuinely does have two
answers and they can disagree — that would be the violation, and it is a small structural detail rather than
an architectural argument.

So the earlier suggestion in this report — *"likely resolution: his arc becomes a caller of the pose API"* —
was half right and stated too strongly. His arc should not be *replaced* by the pose API; it should **emit**
through it, and remain a first-class capability a scene can choose.

## Standing rule this establishes

**Resolve his conflicts against the Laws, not against the compiler.** The `MOONLET_UNIS_N` case is the
example: the compiler was perfectly happy with the wrong answer *and* with the right one, and with the
false-dependency version in between. `docs/00`'s pre-flight is what separated them, and Robin's knowledge of
which path moved to the GPU is what corrected the pre-flight's first answer.
